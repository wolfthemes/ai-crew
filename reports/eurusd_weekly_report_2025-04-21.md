# EUR/USD Weekly Market Report – 2025-04-21

---

## Executive Summary

The EUR/USD pair experienced a decisive bearish shift over the past week, driven by a combination of dovish European Central Bank (ECB) rhetoric, softer Eurozone inflation, and robust US economic data. The pair opened near 1.0780 and trended lower, repeatedly testing the critical 1.0700 support zone. Key drivers included a weaker-than-expected German Manufacturing PMI, a dovish ECB policy statement, and stronger US Consumer Confidence and Nonfarm Payrolls, all of which favored the US dollar. Technicals reinforced the bearish narrative, with a descending triangle pattern and momentum indicators confirming downside pressure.

Looking ahead, the upcoming US Core PCE Price Index and Nonfarm Payrolls are poised to inject further volatility, while ECB President Lagarde’s speech could influence euro sentiment. With the US-EU interest rate differential at multi-year highs and capital flows favoring the dollar, the path of least resistance remains lower for EUR/USD. Traders should monitor the 1.0700 pivot for potential breakdowns, with 1.0660 and 1.0600 as key downside targets. Only a sustained move above 1.0780 would neutralize the immediate bearish bias. Overall, the outlook remains cautious, with risks skewed to the downside barring a significant shift in data or central bank tone.

## Fundamental Analysis

### Monetary Policy Comparison

- **ECB:** Held rates at 3.75% (April 17), with a notably dovish tone. President Lagarde emphasized downside growth risks and faster-than-expected disinflation (Eurozone CPI YoY at 2.4%). The ECB is open to rate cuts if disinflation persists, with no new quantitative tightening announced.
- **Fed:** Maintains a hawkish bias, with the federal funds rate at 5.50%. Recent communications stress “higher for longer” rates, citing persistent inflation and robust economic data. The Fed continues quantitative tightening, draining liquidity and supporting the dollar.
- **Interest Rate Differential:** The US-EU policy rate spread is at a multi-year high (Fed 5.50% vs. ECB 3.75%), underpinning USD strength. Market-implied probabilities for ECB cuts have increased, while the Fed is expected to delay any easing until late 2025.

### Economic Data Analysis

- **GDP Growth:** US GDP growth (~2.1% annualized) outpaces the Eurozone (Q1 2025 at 0.3% YoY). Germany is near recession, weighing on the euro.
- **Inflation:** Eurozone inflation is decelerating (2.4% YoY), close to the ECB’s 2% target. US inflation remains stickier, with Core PCE expected at 0.3% MoM and annualized rates above 2.5%.
- **Employment:** US labor market robust (NFP 182K, unemployment 3.8%). Eurozone unemployment elevated (6.5%), with German manufacturing jobs under pressure.
- **Manufacturing & Services:** German Manufacturing PMI (47.8) remains in contraction. US ISM PMIs are above 50, indicating expansion.
- **Consumer Confidence:** US Consumer Confidence surged to 105.2, while Eurozone sentiment is subdued.

### Geopolitical Factors

- **Euro Risks:** Ongoing uncertainty in Eastern Europe, energy supply vulnerabilities, and political fragmentation within the EU. German exposure to global trade and China’s slowdown adds to risk.
- **Dollar Risks:** USD benefits from safe-haven flows. US political risk is moderate, with no imminent elections or fiscal standoffs.
- **Trade:** Eurozone’s export sector vulnerable to global demand shocks. US trade policy stable.
- **Political Stability:** No immediate election risk in the US; Eurozone faces potential instability from upcoming national elections in Spain and Poland.

### Flow Analysis

- **Capital Flows:** US assets attract capital due to higher yields and economic resilience. Eurozone sees outflows from equities and bonds.
- **Trade Balance:** US trade deficit stable; Eurozone’s surplus narrowing due to weak exports.
- **Portfolio Flows:** US equities and Treasuries attract global inflows; Eurozone assets see tepid demand.
- **Central Bank Reserves:** USD remains the dominant reserve currency; no significant diversification into euros.

### Fundamental Outlook

- **Short-Term (1-2 Weeks):** EUR/USD likely to remain under pressure, with risks skewed to the downside. Upcoming US data (Core PCE, NFP) expected to reinforce the Fed’s hawkish stance. ECB President Lagarde’s speech may offer only limited support unless she signals a policy shift. Technical support at 1.0700 is vulnerable; a break could target 1.0660 or lower.
- **Medium-Term (1-3 Months):** The fundamental backdrop favors continued USD strength. Unless Eurozone data surprises to the upside or the Fed pivots, EUR/USD could test 1.0600 or even 1.0500.

**Key Drivers:**
- US-EU interest rate differential and monetary policy divergence
- Relative economic growth and inflation trends
- Capital flows favoring US assets
- Geopolitical risk premium on the euro

**Potential Price Targets/Ranges:**
- **Short-term:** 1.0660 (initial support), 1.0600 (extension), resistance at 1.0740/1.0780
- **Medium-term:** Downside risk toward 1.0600–1.0500 if bearish momentum persists; upside capped unless ECB/Fed policy outlooks shift materially

**Conclusion:**
The EUR/USD pair is fundamentally biased to the downside, driven by a wide and persistent interest rate differential, stronger US economic data, and dovish ECB signals. Near-term catalysts (US inflation and labor data, ECB communication) are likely to reinforce these trends. Unless there is a significant shift in Eurozone data or Fed policy, rallies are likely to be sold, with 1.0700 as a key pivot and 1.0600 as the next major target.
