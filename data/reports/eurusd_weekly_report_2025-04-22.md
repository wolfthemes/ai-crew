# EUR/USD Weekly Market Report – 2025-04-22

---

## Executive Summary

The EUR/USD pair experienced a dramatic surge over the past week, breaking decisively above the psychologically significant 1.1500 level for the first time since November 2021. This move was driven by a confluence of fundamental and technical factors, most notably the European Central Bank’s (ECB) dovish rate cut on April 17 and a sharp US Dollar sell-off following President Trump’s public criticism of the Federal Reserve’s independence. Despite the ECB’s easing, the Euro displayed remarkable resilience, buoyed by market confidence in the central bank’s credibility and a rotation away from the USD amid heightened US political risk.

Technically, EUR/USD staged a textbook bullish breakout, with momentum indicators confirming trend acceleration but also warning of overbought conditions. Sentiment is overwhelmingly bullish among institutional and speculative traders, while retail traders are heavily short—a classic contrarian signal that supports further upside in the near term.

Looking ahead, the upcoming week’s high-impact PMI releases for both the Eurozone and the US (April 23) are poised to inject fresh volatility. While the medium-term outlook remains bullish, traders should be alert to the risk of a short-term pullback if sentiment becomes excessively stretched or if economic data disappoints. Overall, the path of least resistance remains higher, with 1.1620–1.1700 as plausible targets, provided 1.1400 support holds.

## Technical Analysis

### Price Action Analysis

Over the past week, EUR/USD staged a decisive bullish breakout, surging from the 1.1380 region to highs above 1.1550. The rally was catalyzed by the ECB’s dovish rate cut and accelerated by a sharp US Dollar sell-off following political pressure on the Fed. Price action featured strong bullish daily candles, including a Marubozu bar on April 17, confirming aggressive buying interest.

**Key Price Levels Tested/Broken:**
- **1.1400:** Clean break on April 17, with strong bullish momentum.
- **1.1500:** Psychological and multi-year resistance, decisively breached on April 22.
- **1.1550:** Intraday high, marking the upper bound of the current rally.

**Chart Patterns:**
- **Ascending Triangle Breakout:** The move resembles a bullish continuation breakout from a multi-week ascending triangle, with 1.1500 as the horizontal resistance.
- **Bullish Flag/Pennant:** On the 1H and 4H charts, a bullish flag formed during April 18–21 consolidation, resolving higher on April 22.

**Candlestick Patterns:**
- **April 17:** Bullish Marubozu (full-bodied candle, no wicks), confirming strong buying.
- **April 18–21:** Series of small-bodied candles (dojis/spinning tops) near 1.1500, indicating indecision and consolidation.
- **April 22:** Bullish engulfing candle on the 4H chart, signaling renewed upside momentum.

## Fundamental Analysis

### Monetary Policy Comparison

**ECB:**
- Cut deposit rate to 2.75% (April 17), dovish but data-dependent.
- Balance sheet shrinking at a measured pace; no new QE.
- Market confidence in ECB’s ability to manage downside risks.

**Fed:**
- Policy rate steady at 4.75%, but under intense political pressure.
- President Trump’s attacks on Fed independence have raised concerns about policy credibility.
- Slower pace of quantitative tightening; forward guidance ambiguous.

**Interest Rate Differentials:**
- EUR/USD rate differential has narrowed to ~200bps, lowest since 2021.
- Markets price a 60% chance of a Fed rate cut by June 2025; ECB expected to pause further cuts.
- Narrowing differential is a key driver of EUR/USD strength.

### Economic Data Analysis

**GDP Growth:**
- US: 1.8% annualized (Q1 2025), down from 2.3% (Q4 2024).
- Eurozone: 0.7% annualized (Q1 2025), up from 0.3% (Q4 2024).
- Growth gap narrowing, supporting Euro resilience.

**Inflation:**
- US CPI: 2.5% YoY (March 2025).
- Eurozone CPI: 2.2% YoY.
- Core inflation trending lower in both regions; urgency for further rate cuts reduced.

**Employment:**
- US unemployment: 4.1%, up from 3.8%.
- Eurozone unemployment: 6.2%, stable.
- Labor markets cooling, with US showing more signs of deceleration.

**Manufacturing & Services:**
- Both regions face sluggish manufacturing; services remain resilient.
- Upcoming PMIs (April 23) are critical for near-term direction.

**Consumer Confidence:**
- US: Conference Board index at 92 (lower).
- Eurozone: European Commission index at -9.5 (improving).

### Geopolitical Factors

**Eurozone:**
- Moderate geopolitical headwinds (Ukraine, energy), but improved political stability.
- No major elections or fiscal crises on the horizon.

**US:**
- Domestic political instability and attacks on Fed independence have rattled markets.
- US-China trade tensions resurfacing.
- USD’s safe-haven appeal diminished.

**Trade Dynamics:**
- US threatening new tariffs on European autos; negotiations ongoing.
- Eurozone’s trade surplus with US remains a flashpoint.

### Flow Analysis

**Capital Flows:**
- Rotation out of US assets; foreign investors reducing Treasury holdings.
- Eurozone bond inflows increasing.

**Trade Balance:**
- Eurozone: Current account surplus (~2.5% of GDP).
- US: Persistent deficit (~3.2% of GDP).

**Portfolio Flows:**
- Eurozone equities attracting inflows; US equity outflows accelerating.

**Central Bank Reserves:**
- Gradual increase in Euro allocations at the expense of USD.

### Fundamental Outlook

**Short-Term (1–2 Weeks):**
- EUR/USD likely to consolidate above 1.1500, with volatility around April 23 PMI releases.
- Upside risk if Eurozone PMIs surprise or US PMIs disappoint.

**Medium-Term (1–3 Months):**
- Further EUR/USD upside favored if ECB pauses cuts and Fed’s independence remains in question.
- Move toward 1.1700 plausible if US risks persist and Eurozone data stabilizes.

**Key Drivers:**
- US political risk and Fed independence concerns (USD negative).
- Narrowing rate differential (EUR positive).
- Eurozone economic stabilization and structural flows (EUR positive).
- Geopolitical risks (USD negative, EUR neutral).

**Potential Price Targets:**
- **Short-term:** `1.1550–1.1620`
- **Medium-term:** `1.1620–1.1700`
- **Downside:** `1.1470–1.1400` (if Eurozone data disappoints or US risks abate)

**Summary:** The EUR/USD rally is fundamentally justified by monetary policy convergence, US political risk, and improving Eurozone sentiment. While overbought technicals warn of near-term consolidation, the medium-term outlook remains bullish.
