## 3. Fundamental Outlook

- **ECB:** Remains in a cautious tightening cycle, with inflation above target and a hawkish bias, but no policy meeting this week. Market attention will be on Eurozone data and any unscheduled ECB commentary.
- **Fed:** In a data-dependent pause. No rate change is expected at the May 7 FOMC, but the statement and Powell’s press conference are pivotal. The Fed is balancing sticky inflation with signs of slowing growth. The tone of forward guidance will be the main driver for USD direction.

**Interest Rate Differential:** The Fed’s rate premium over the ECB has narrowed as the ECB hikes and the Fed pauses, mildly supporting EUR/USD.

### Economic Growth Dynamics

- **US:** Growth is slowing, with Q1 GDP softer than expected and consumer spending moderating.
- **Eurozone:** Growth is sluggish but stabilizing, with recent data showing modest expansion and retail sales in focus this week.

### Inflation Outlook

- **US:** Inflation is moderating but remains above target; wage growth is slowing.
- **Eurozone:** Inflation is also easing, but core remains sticky, supporting the ECB’s hawkish stance.

### Geopolitical Factors

No acute geopolitical risks are present, but energy dependency and the US election cycle are medium-term watchpoints.

**Fundamental Bias Statement:**  
Short-term, EUR/USD is likely to remain volatile and rangebound (1.1265–1.1347/1.1425), with direction dictated by the Fed’s tone and data surprises. The narrowing policy and growth differentials, combined with technical consolidation, tilt the bias slightly bullish, but conviction is low until after the Fed. Medium-term, the bias is mildly bullish EUR/USD, barring a hawkish Fed or Eurozone data miss.
